### **Abstract:** 

**C2S -** A Smart Garment Factory Management System capable of automating various factory activities. It is a system that combines performance monitoring, safety reporting, factory simulation, quality management, equipment maintenance, scheduling, and inventory management. It provides all these functions using a smart management system. 

This technology improves the productivity and accuracy of factory output and processing through the provision of real-time information and analysis by artificial intelligence. The system decreases the need for human input and the associated risks of delays and poor working conditions.

### **CHAPTER 1: Introduction** 

###### **1.1 Purpose:** 

The aim of this project is to design and propose an AI assistant Factory Worker Management and Production Optimization System that works towards ensuring proper remuneration of factory workers, enhancing factory safety and ensuring efficient production is achieved in a small and medium scale factory. 

Many factories today use manual processes in assessing employees, payroll, safety, and production planning, which in most cases translates to inefficiency and losses. The proposed system will therefore focus on automating these processes to ensure efficiency and accountability. The system still looks to be effective and efficient for small business ventures. 

###### **1.2 Problem Statement:** 

Small and medium sized factories have many challenges performing their operations concerning worker management, production efficiency and financial management. The lack of a comprehensive digital platform causes problems such as  unfair remuneration, insufficient safety monitoring, inefficient production planning, and resource wastage 

###### **1.2.1 Problem Background:** 

In many emerging manufacturing systems, particularly in related garments or light Industries, factory process controls utilise paper documentation systems or unrefined computer systems. Worker remuneration rates tend to be subjective,  safety problems remain unreported either because of communication constraints or fear, and manufacturing process decisions tend to be poorly forecast or simulated. 

Therefore, both laborers and factory owners are adversely affected. Laborers are poorly treated and are not provided a safety environment, while owners are faced with decreased production, losses and uncertainty. 

###### **1.2.2 Problem Description:** 

The code issues dealt with within the project are: 

- Discriminating remuneration of the workers owing to the absence of optimal assessment of their performance. 

- Inadequate processes for reporting worker safety issues particularly for non English speaking employees. 

- Inaccurate production planning resulting in delays and failure to achieve targets. 

- High defects and waste because of insufficient analysis of quality control and unplanned machine failures leading to downtime and higher maintenance expenses. 

- Challenges facing auditors and audited businesses when preparing for audits and reporting. 

- Poor demand forecasting and inventory management, leading to overstocking or inventory shortages. 

These challenges are interwound, therefore not easily comprehended in disconnected systems. 

###### **1.2.3 Problem Reasoning:** 

The reasons for such problems reside in the absence of an intelligent management system suitable for small and medium scale factories. The current situation in this area is that management methods are normally 

- Too expensive 

- Too complex 

- Design only for large companies 

Without real time data, predictive analytics, or worker friendly interfaces, factories just don't have anything better than guesswork to go on, so they’re stuck in inefficient systems they don't trust. 

###### **1.3 Goal:** 

The main objective of this work is to develop a centralised AI assisted system that: 

- Ensure the rating of workers is fair and biased as well as their payment. 

- Provides a Bangla-based safety reporting system for workers. 

- Improves quality control and waste reduction. 

- It enables predictive maintenance of machines. 

- It is easy to do compliance and buyer audit reporting. 

- Supports financial planning, inventory control, and sequencing of jobs. 

- It brings more transparency, productivity and sustainability to small and medium factories.

### **CHAPTER 3: System Analysis** 

###### **3.1 Introduction:** 

System analysis involves an important stage where the existing solutions are assessed and the gaps between the existing systems and the requirements of the users are identified. In this chapter, the gaps in the existing factory and manufacturing management systems have been analyzed. 

It is aimed at worker fairness,safety, usability ,and affordability in a small factory,which are normally neglected while implementing ERP in traditional systems. After this analysis, a gap analysis and benchmarking are done. 

###### **3.2 Gap Analysis:** 

Targeted Gap Analysis: What Existing Systems Lack and What Our System Offers 

Most of the current existing systems of ERP and factory management in Bangladesh are designed with a focus on large sized factories, and their inclination was not towards worker centric fairness and accessibility. The gaps that were identified are: 

###### **1. Worker Performance & Fair Payment** 

###### **● Existing Systems:** 

- Anecdotal limited transparency in worker evaluation 

- Incentive and wage calculation is often done manually. Presence of bias in performance evaluation 

###### **● Our System :** 

Transparent, ratings-based performance appraisal 

Performance-linked incentives and wage calculation 

Clear visibility to workers and management alike 

###### **2. Worker Safety & Reporting** 

###### **● Existing Systems:** 

Safety reporting is mostly either totally absent or highly formal. 

Language barriers are a limiting factor to worker participation. 

- **Our System:** 

   - A simple, Bangla-based safety reporting interface 

   - Encourages participation by the workers and early reporting of any issues. 

###### **3. Production Planning & Forecasting** 

- **Existing Systems:** 

   - Pay more attention to historical reporting, rather than early prediction. This would limit simulations for minor factories. 

- **Our System:** 

   - Early production output prediction 

Production simulation to support better planning decisions 

###### **4. Small Business Usability** 

- **Existing Systems:** 

   - Complex interfaces containing technical vocabulary High implementation and training costs 

###### **● Our System:** 

- Clean, intuitive UI targeted toward non-technical users. Suitable for small and medium factories 

###### **5. Machine Health & Downtime** 

- **Existing Systems:** 

   - Reactive maintenance approaches 

   - Usually, downtime analysis is done post-failure. 

- **Our System:** 

   - Predictive maintenance indicators 

Early notifications to reduce downtime and maintenance cost 

###### **6. Compliance & Audit Support** 

###### **● Existing Systems:** 

Manual audit preparation 

Limited automation for buyer inspections 

###### **● Our System:** 

- Automate compliance and audit report generation. 

- Saving time for the owners of the factories when it comes to inspections.

- Unlike traditional systems that rely on complex forms or English-based interfaces, this system allows workers to report safety concerns in Bangla. This language accessibility encourages workers to report hazards without fear or confusion, leading to early detection of safety risks and improved workplace safety. 

- It assumes that the users have basic digital skills and device access, such as smartphones or computers. In factories where digital literacy is generally low, the initial adoption, without proper training, can be very difficult.

- To function effectively, the system requires accurate initial data entry related to workers, machines, production lines, and inventory. This setup phase may require additional time and effort from factory staff, which could slow early adoption.

### **Chapter - 5: Prototype or UI Design** 

###### **5.1 Introduction:** 

This chapter describes the prototype and UI design of the C2S ( A Garments Management system).The system is designed to simplify and manage garments factory operations such as production, inventory, orders, and employee records. The main goal of the UI design is to create a simple, efficient, and user-friendly interface for administrators and staff.This chapter also highlights the tools used for prototyping, key UI components, and the overall sitemap of the system. The proposed UI ensures smooth navigation, clear data presentation, and improved workflow management within the garments industry. 

###### **5.2 Prototyping Tools Used:** 

The following resources were used to guarantee a functional and aesthetically pleasing prototype: 

1. Figma: For producing interactive prototypes and high-fidelity wireframes. The design team was able to get comments in real time because of Figma's collaborative features. 

2. Canva: Facilitated the production of graphic materials, including promotional images and symbols. 

###### **5.3 UI Design:** 

In order to satisfy the needs of its intended audience, Project C2S’s user interface design prioritizes accessibility, simplicity, and customization. Key components of the design include the following: 

###### **Rules of UI Design:** 

To specify best practices for efficient user interface design, a number of well-known guideline frameworks have been put out, including Norman's Design Principles, Shneiderman's Eight Golden Rules, and the Three Golden Rules. 

###### **Mandel's Golden Rules:** 

These rules fall into three major categories: 

- Give Users Authority 

- Reduce the Memory Load of Users 

- Preserve Interface Uniformity 

A set of particular guidelines that direct the design process are included in each area. The rules, along with a key concept for each group, are outlined below. 

###### **Place Users in Control** 

- Prefer modeless interactions and use interface modes with caution. 

- Allow for versatile operation by supporting both keyboard and mouse inputs. 

- Make it simple for users to pause ongoing actions or change their focus (interruptible). 

- Provide labels and statements that are clear, instructive, and useful. 

- Give prompt feedback and permit actions to be reversed or rectified (forgiving) 

- Make sure there are well-defined routes and exit options for easy navigation. 

- Create a system that is accessible to people with different levels of experience. 

- To make tasks easier to complete, keep the interface clear and uncomplicated. 

- Permit customization choices to accommodate each user's preferences 

- Permit direct interaction with objects and elements on the screen (interactive) 

###### **Reduce Users’ Memory Load** 

- Reduce the amount of information that users must retain. 

- Create user interfaces that prioritize recognition over recall. 

- Make use of obvious visual cues and indicators (inform) 

- Provide default settings in addition to redo and undo capabilities (forgiving) 

- Give frequently used actions shortcuts (frequency) 

- Promote an intuitive, natural object-action interaction style. 

- Use metaphors and well-known real-world concepts (transfer) 

- Information should be gradually revealed in accordance with user demands (context). 

- Keep your visual layout tidy and well-structured. 

###### **5.3.1 Color Palette** 

Primary Colors: To provide maximum contrast and readability, black, white, and yellow are utilized as the major colors to create a clean, polished, and contemporary appearance. 

Secondary Colors: To give the interface visual harmony and a serene, user-friendly vibe, blue tones and delicate pastel hues are used as secondary colors. 

Accent Colors: To draw attention to key components like buttons, alerts, and call-to-action elements, yellow is employed as the accent color. 

###### **5.3.2 Typography** 

Headings: To guarantee a clear hierarchy and powerful visual emphasis, the Roboto font's bold weight is employed for headings. 

Body Text: To preserve readability and a neat, polished appearance, Roboto typeface is used in both regular and light weights for body text. 

###### **5.3.3 Layout** 

**Navigation:** To guarantee easy and simple navigation, a bottom navigation bar with five main tabs is designed: 

Home: Offers a summary of the system and emphasizes important details. About Us: Explains the organization's goals. 

Services: Shows the system's features and services that are available. Features: Highlights the system's primary tools and features. Contact  us: enables users to contact them via a feedback form or contact information. 

**Card-Based Interface:** A card-based structure is used to convey actions and information, making it simple to scan and interact with the content. 

The UI is completely responsive, guaranteeing uniform use across desktop, tablet, and smartphone devices. 

###### **5.3.4 Key Features** 

###### **Worker Performance and Rating System:** 

The system includes a worker performance and rating module that monitors individual productivity, work quality, and task completion. Performance ratings are generated based on predefined criteria, allowing management to evaluate workers fairly and consistently. 

###### **Performance-Based Payment System:** 

A performance-based payment mechanism is integrated into the system, where worker compensation is calculated according to performance scores, productivity levels, and completed work. This approach encourages efficiency, accountability, and improved overall output. 

###### **Worker Reporting System:** 

The worker reporting system enables supervisors to generate detailed reports on worker performance, attendance, and payment history. These reports support transparency, informed decision-making, and effective workforce management.
